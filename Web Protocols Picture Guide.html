Web Protocols Picture Guide
Explain it like I'm five

Six web protocols, in pictures

A protocol is a set of rules both computers agree on. Most of these come in pairs: the plain version and the locked, safe version.

Two rules that always work as a team.

IPWrites the address on every piece, so it reaches the right computer.
TCPCuts the message into numbered pieces, checks they all arrived, and puts them back in order.

Like: posting a big jigsaw puzzle in numbered envelopes. IP is the address. TCP is the numbers and the "did it all arrive?" check.

DNS

You know the name. Computers need the number. DNS looks it up.

Like: your phone's contact list. You tap "Mum", it dials the number. Port 53

HTTP vs HTTPS

How your browser asks a website for a page, and gets it back.

HTTPopenA postcard. Anyone on the way can read it.Port 80
HTTPSlockedA sealed envelope. Only the website can open it.Port 443

SSL vs TLS

This is the lock itself. It is what puts the "S" in HTTPS.

SSLretiredThe first lock. It has known holes. Don't use it.
TLSuse thisThe newer, stronger lock. Used today.

People still say "SSL certificate" out of habit. They really mean TLS.

FTP vs SFTP

Moving whole files from one computer to another, like uploading a website.

FTPopenOpen truck. Files and even your password can be seen.Ports 20, 21
SFTPlockedLocked truck. Everything is scrambled on the way.Port 22

Email uses one rule to send and a choice of two to collect.

SMTPSends your email to the mail server. Think "SMTP = Send".Port 25 / 587
POP3Takes the mail home to one device and empties the box. Other devices can't see it.Port 110 / 995 locked
IMAPMail stays on the server. Phone and laptop see the same inbox.Port 143 / 993 locked

Like: SMTP is the postie. POP3 is emptying your letterbox into your bag. IMAP is reading your letters at the post office from any branch.
